About HERD

The Higher Education Research and Development (HERD) Survey is the main source of data on research and development spending at U.S. colleges and universities. The National Center for Science and Engineering Statistics (NCSES), part of the U.S. National Science Foundation, runs it every year. In FY 2024, institutions reported their R&D spending to the survey.

The survey

  • Who reports. Public and private nonprofit colleges and universities in the United States and its territories that grant a bachelor's degree or higher and spend at least $150,000 on separately accounted-for R&D in their fiscal year. Each geographically separate campus headed by its own president or chancellor reports on its own. The survey is a census of these institutions, not a sample.
  • What counts as R&D. Creative and systematic work undertaken to increase knowledge: basic research, applied research and experimental development, in every field, not only science and engineering. Spending includes direct costs, indirect costs (recovered and unrecovered) and money passed through to other institutions on subawards. It leaves out public service and outreach, curriculum development, training grants not for research, and construction or renovation.
  • What it asks. R&D spending by source of funds (federal, state and local government, the institution itself, business, nonprofits and others), by federal agency, by field of research and by type of cost, plus subawards given and received and counts of R&D personnel. Institutions with less than $1 million in R&D answer a short form with a few core questions.
  • When. Each institution reports for its own fiscal year, which for most runs July 1 to June 30. NCSES collects the data online over the following school year and publishes them roughly a year and a half after the fiscal year ends.
  • History. HERD replaced the Survey of Research and Development Expenditures at Universities and Colleges, which ran from FY 1972, in FY 2010, adding questions and campus-level reporting. The short form began in FY 2012, which is why this site starts there.
  • Who uses it. Universities benchmark against peers with it, NCSES uses it for national statistics such as Science and Engineering Indicators, and it underlies rankings and classifications, including the R&D spending measure in the Carnegie research designations.

Things to keep in mind

  • Institutions report from their own accounting records, so what counts as separately accounted-for R&D, and especially institution funds, varies somewhat from one institution to another.
  • When an institution does not answer, NCSES imputes its values from its earlier years and its peers. Imputed amounts are a small share of the national total (well under 1 percent).
  • Subaward money is counted twice in national totals: once by the institution that passes it through and again by the one that spends it.
  • R&D at federally funded research and development centers (FFRDCs) that universities manage is covered by a separate NCSES survey, not by HERD.

NCSES describes the survey, its questionnaire and its methods in full on the HERD Survey page.

How the data were put together

  • Years. FY 2012 to FY 2024. All of these years use the same survey design, introduced in FY 2010, and include the short-form survey for institutions with less than $1 million in R&D, so national totals are complete.
  • Checked against NCSES. Each build compares 15 national totals in every year with the tables NCSES publishes (NSF 26-304, Tables 2 and 12). All match exactly; the build stops if any do not.
  • Two survey forms. Institutions with $1 million or more in R&D answer the standard form; smaller ones answer a short form with fewer questions. Measures available only on the standard form are labeled that way below, and their national totals leave out short-form institutions (about 0.1 percent of R&D).
  • Not released by institution. NCSES does not publish, for individual institutions, how institution funds split into institutionally financed research, cost sharing and unrecovered indirect costs, or how much indirect cost was recovered. Those appear on this site only as national totals from the published tables.
  • Personnel counts are released from FY 2022 onward.
  • Institution type comes from the 2021 Carnegie Classification, held fixed for every year so a group means the same institutions throughout. HERD reporting units without a usable IPEDS ID (combined campuses, system offices, research institutes) are matched by a hand-checked crosswalk. Seven independent research institutes and three system offices, which Carnegie does not classify, are labeled by HERD Explorer.
  • Locations for the chart builder's map come from the IPEDS directory file for the year closest to each institution's last HERD year. Units without an IPEDS record are placed from their street address by a hand-checked crosswalk.
  • Imputed values. NCSES imputes some values for nonresponse; the survey-values download keeps NCSES's status code for every value (i = imputed by NCSES). HERD Explorer does not impute or fill in any values.
  • Dollars are nominal (not adjusted for inflation).

How projections are made

Projections come from a hierarchical growth model fit with the R package mgcv, simulated 200 times per institution and tested against the past. A projection shows where past trends lead if they continue. It does not account for known upcoming changes, such as federal budget or policy shifts, and may not reflect what actually happens. About the model spells out the approach, the testing and the limitations.
